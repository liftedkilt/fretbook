# Practice exercises

Fretbook can write practice exercises for you. Open **Tools → Exercises**, choose a type, adjust the settings and the tab appears at once. **Save to library** stores it as an ordinary song, so every player mode (playback, loop, speed trainer, wait mode, scored play) works on it.

## What it can generate

| Type | Settings |
| --- | --- |
| Scale or mode | Key; major, the church modes, minor, harmonic and melodic minor, pentatonics, blues, whole tone; position; range; direction; rhythm |
| Arpeggio | Any chord symbol Fretbook knows, such as `Am7` or `F#m7b5`; position, range, direction and rhythm as above |
| Finger drill | Start fret, finger order (`1234`, `1324`, …), low-to-high or high-to-low across the strings, and how many times to shift up a fret |
| Chord changes | Two to four chords, beats per chord, a strumming pattern and how many times through |
| Bass pattern | Up to eight chords, one bar each: root-fifth-octave or a walking line that approaches each next root by a half step |

Every exercise follows the instrument and tuning you pick (4-, 5- or 7-string, drop D and so on), the tempo, and the rhythm: quarter notes, eighths, triplets or sixteenths.

## Positions

A position is a window of five frets (index finger on the first, little finger on the fifth), so a scale never stretches beyond a comfortable hand span. Pick it three ways:

- **From a fret** puts the window at the fret you choose.
- **CAGED shape** places the window where the C, A, G, E or D chord shape sits for the key. A mode uses the shape of its parent major scale (D Dorian sits where C major does).
- **Lowest position** picks the lowest window that holds the key's note.

**Range** is the whole position, or one or two octaves from the tonic.

## Preset drills

The chips at the top load ready-made drills: a pentatonic box, a major octave, Dorian triplets, an Am7 arpeggio, two spider drills, open chord changes, a folk strum, root-fifth-octave and a walking blues bass line. They only change the form, so you can adjust them before saving.

## Notes

- Strumming patterns are written as chord beats; the player sounds all the notes together, and an up-stroke uses only the top three strings.
- Exercises are tagged `exercise` when saved.
